EXAM 01 / FOUNDATION / LOGIC

Logic and iff

FoundationLogic

Logic and iff

20 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

Prove for each integer n: n is odd if and only if n² is odd. Label the two directions.

Reveal the worked proof

If n=2k+1, then n²=2(2k²+2k)+1 is odd. Conversely if n² is odd, n cannot be even, as n=2k would imply n²=4k² even. Both implications are proved.

QUESTION 2

Find all real x with x²=4x. Show why the list is complete and verify every candidate.

Reveal the worked proof

Factor x(x−4)=0, so x=0 or x=4. These both satisfy x²=4x; there are no others because the factorisation is equivalent.

EXAM 02 / FOUNDATION / FUNCTIONS

Functions, integer domain

FoundationFunctions

Functions, integer domain

25 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

Find all f:ℤ→ℤ satisfying f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y) for all integers x,y and f(1)=3.

Reveal the worked proof

Set x=y=0 to show f(0)=0. From x+(−x)=0 get f(−x)=−f(x). For n≥1, induction gives f(n)=3n, and negative n follows. Conversely 3(x+y)=3x+3y.

QUESTION 2

A function f:ℤ→ℤ obeys f(n+2)=f(n)+4, f(0)=1 and f(1)=0. Determine every f(n).

Reveal the worked proof

For each integer k, f(2k)=1+4k and f(2k+1)=4k, using forward and backward induction in steps of two. Both formulas satisfy the recurrence and initial values and are unique.

EXAM 03 / BRIDGE / NUMBER THEORY

GCD and residue classes

BridgeNumber theory

GCD and residue classes

25 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

For every integer n, determine gcd(n²+1,n+1), and prove both cases.

Reveal the worked proof

Modulo n+1, n≡−1 gives n²+1≡2; any common divisor divides 2. If n odd, both n²+1 and n+1 are even, so gcd=2; if n even, n+1 odd so gcd=1.

QUESTION 2

Prove that x²+y²=4k+3 has no integer solutions, where k is any integer.

Reveal the worked proof

Every square is 0 or 1 modulo 4, so x²+y² is 0,1,or2 modulo4, never3. This is exhaustive, including negative integers.

EXAM 04 / BRIDGE / GEOMETRY

Circle geometry foundations

BridgeGeometry

Circle geometry foundations

30 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

Two chords AC and BD of a circle meet at an interior point P. Prove PA·PC=PB·PD without merely quoting a formula.

Reveal the worked proof

Prove △PAB∼△PDC using ∠PAB=∠PDC and ∠PBA=∠PCD as cyclic angles. Corresponding ratios PA/PD=PB/PC yield the product identity.

QUESTION 2

In △ABC, D lies on BC and AD bisects ∠BAC. Prove BD/DC=AB/AC.

Reveal the worked proof

Triangles ABD and ACD share altitude from A to BC, so their area ratio is BD/DC. Using area=½·side·side·sin(included angle), their ratio is (AB·AD·sin BAD)/(AC·AD·sin DAC)=AB/AC because the bisected angles are equal. Equate the ratios.

EXAM 05 / FOUNDATION / COMBINATORICS

Counting without duplicates

FoundationCombinatorics

Counting without duplicates

25 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

Choose any five distinct integers from {1,…,8}. Prove two of them sum to 9.

Reveal the worked proof

The four disjoint complementary pairs (1,8),(2,7),(3,6),(4,5) partition the eight integers. Five selections force two from one pair by pigeonhole.

QUESTION 2

How many nonincreasing sequences of length four can be formed with scores 0,1,2,3? Justify.

Reveal the worked proof

Let x₀,x₁,x₂,x₃ count score multiplicities. They are nonnegative and sum to 4. Stars and bars gives C(4+4−1,4−1)=C(7,3)=35. The imposed decreasing order makes each distribution correspond to exactly one sequence.

EXAM 06 / BRIDGE / ALGEBRA

Inequalities and equality

BridgeAlgebra

Inequalities and equality

30 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

If a,b,c are nonnegative and a+b+c=1, prove a²+b²+c²≥1/3 and classify equality.

Reveal the worked proof

Cauchy–Schwarz: (a+b+c)²≤3(a²+b²+c²). The sum is 1, so Σa²≥1/3. Equality iff a=b=c, giving a=b=c=1/3; verify.

QUESTION 2

For all real x,y, prove xy≤(x+y)²/4 and identify equality.

Reveal the worked proof

(x−y)²≥0 implies x²−2xy+y²≥0, hence (x+y)²≥4xy. Equality iff x=y. No positivity is required for this version.

EXAM 07 / BRIDGE / ALGEBRA

Diophantine classification

BridgeAlgebra

Diophantine classification

30 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

Find every integer pair (x,y) satisfying x²−y²=15, explaining the signs and parity.

Reveal the worked proof

Set u=x−y, v=x+y so uv=15; all signed ordered divisor pairs are odd, hence x=(u+v)/2 and y=(v−u)/2 are integers. Enumerate (u,v)=(±1,±15),(±3,±5),(±5,±3),(±15,±1) with simultaneous signs. The resulting pairs are (±8,±7) and (±4,±1), with independent y signs matched by these factors; check each of eight.

QUESTION 2

Prove n(n+1) is even for every integer n.

Reveal the worked proof

Two consecutive integers have opposite parity, so at least one is even. Their product is therefore even; includes negative n and zero.

EXAM 08 / BRIDGE / PROOF CRAFT

Induction and exact statements

BridgeProof craft

Induction and exact statements

30 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

Prove by induction: 1+3+5+···+(2n−1)=n² for every n≥1.

Reveal the worked proof

Base n=1:1=1. If sum through n is n², adding next odd number 2n+1 gives n²+2n+1=(n+1)². Thus by induction all n.

QUESTION 2

Prove 2ⁿ≥n+1 for every integer n≥0 and determine all equality cases.

Reveal the worked proof

Base n=0:1=1. If 2ⁿ≥n+1, then 2ⁿ⁺¹≥2n+2≥n+2 for n≥0. Equality at n=0 and n=1; for n≥1, the second inequality is strict when n>0, hence n≥2 strict.

EXAM 09 / ADVANCED / COMBINATORICS

Sharpness and subsequences

AdvancedCombinatorics

Sharpness and subsequences

35 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

Prove every five distinct real numbers contain an increasing or decreasing subsequence of length three. Exhibit four terms avoiding both.

Reveal the worked proof

At each index i record (pᵢ,qᵢ) longest increasing/decreasing subsequence lengths ending there. If both at most2, there are only four states. Five indices force i<j with same state; distinct numbers force one length at j to exceed the matching value, contradiction. Example 2,1,4,3 avoids length3 both ways.

QUESTION 2

Show every two-colouring of a 3×7 grid has a monochromatic rectangle. Is seven optimal?

Reveal the worked proof

Each column has two equal-colour cells, giving a label (one of 3 row pairs, one of 2 colours), six labels. Seven columns repeat a label and create a monochromatic rectangle. For six columns take the 3 patterns with exactly two black cells and the 3 patterns with exactly two white cells, one of each possible row pair. No label repeats and no monochromatic rectangle forms.

EXAM 10 / ADVANCED / FUNCTIONS

Functional equations: next step

AdvancedFunctions

Functional equations: next step

30 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

Find f:ℤ→ℤ satisfying f(x+y)=f(x)+f(y)+2xy for all integers and f(1)=1.

Reveal the worked proof

Put g(n)=f(n)−n². Then g(x+y)=g(x)+g(y), and g(1)=0. Integer additivity forces g(n)=0 for all integers, so f(n)=n². Verify the original identity.

QUESTION 2

Find f:ℤ→ℤ satisfying f(n+1)+f(n−1)=2f(n), f(0)=2, f(1)=5.

Reveal the worked proof

The differences f(n)−f(n−1) are constant, hence f(n)=an+b for all integers. From f(0)=2, b=2; f(1)=5 gives a=3. Verify f(n)=3n+2 satisfies the recurrence.

EXAM 11 / ADVANCED / COMBINATORICS

Invariants and impossibility

AdvancedCombinatorics

Invariants and impossibility

35 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

An 8×8 chessboard loses its two opposite corners. Prove it cannot be tiled by 1×2 dominoes.

Reveal the worked proof

Opposite corners have the same colour; deleting them leaves unequal counts of black and white squares. Every domino covers one of each. A tiling would cover equal counts, contradiction.

QUESTION 2

A pair of integers starts at (0,2). Each move changes (a,b) to (a+2,b−2). Can it ever become (1,1)? Prove.

Reveal the worked proof

The parity of a is invariant: a starts even and each move changes it by an even integer. At (1,1), a would be odd, contradiction. Sum a+b=2 is also invariant but not sufficient to rule out the target by itself.

EXAM 12 / STRETCH / MIXED

International entry stretch

StretchMixed

International entry stretch

45 minutes suggested
Two proof questions · no auto-mark

QUESTION 1

Prove no integers x,y satisfy x²−2y²=3.

Reveal the worked proof

Every square modulo 8 is 0,1,or4. Twice a square modulo8 is 0 or2. Their difference can only be 0,1,2,4,6 or7 modulo8, never3. Thus no integer solutions exist.

QUESTION 2

For each positive integer n, prove that any n+1 distinct integers from 1 to 2n contain two numbers such that one divides the other.

Reveal the worked proof

Write every chosen integer uniquely as 2ᵏm with m odd. There are exactly n possible odd cores m in {1,3,…,2n−1}. Among n+1 chosen integers, two have the same odd core and so are two powers of 2 times that core. The smaller divides the larger.
