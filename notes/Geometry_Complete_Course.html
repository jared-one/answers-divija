01 / DIAGRAM DISCIPLINE

What a diagram may and may not prove

Draw accurately to discover a relationship, but an observed right angle or equal length is not a proof. Name every constructed point and the property defining it.

If A,B,C,D,E lie on a circle, an extra F is allowed, but “F lies on the circle and on this line” requires a construction establishing the intersection. If an intersection might be tangent, at infinity or nonexistent, check the case.

Introduce no unexplained diagram labels in your final write-up. Mark equalities only after deriving them or when they are given.

02 / CIRCLES

Angles in the same segment and cyclic converses

Four points A,B,C,D lie on a circle ⇒∠ABC and ∠ADC sum to 180° when they are opposite angles of a convex cyclic quadrilateral. Conversely, if those opposite angles are supplementary, the points are concyclic (with the correct configuration).

∠ABC + ∠ADC = 180° ⇔ A,B,C,D concyclic (convex case).

When points may lie on extensions or across a chord, directed angles modulo 180° provide a cleaner statement. Beware of applying an unsigned-angle equality to the wrong side of a segment.

03 / STRUCTURE

Triangle similarity and correspondence

AA similarity requires two pairs of corresponding equal angles. State the vertex correspondence before ratios: “△PAB∼△PDC with P↔P, A↔D, B↔C.” Then PA/PD=PB/PC and PA·PC=PB·PD.

Do not pick a visually convenient ratio first and retrofit similarity. The wrong correspondence can produce a false equality even when both triangles really are similar.

04 / CHORDS

Power of a point, derived

If chords AC and BD meet inside a circle at P, the two cyclic-angle equalities yield △PAB∼△PDC. Hence PA·PC=PB·PD. This identity is called the power-of-a-point relation, but a proof can derive it without naming it.

PA × PC = PB × PD.

For an exterior P, secant products and tangent-square forms follow from similar triangles, with directed lengths or appropriate positive segment conventions. Identify inside versus outside before writing the formula.

05 / ORTHOCENTRES

Reflected orthocentre and circumcircle

In acute △ABC with orthocentre H, reflect H across BC to H′. Use BH⊥AC, CH⊥AB to derive ∠BHC=180°−∠BAC. Reflection preserves the angle ∠BH′C. Because H′ lies across BC from A, the supplementary-angle criterion shows A,B,H′,C are concyclic.

Repeat by vertex relabelling for AB and CA. This proof is shorter than constructing several additional circles and hoping their intersections provide the conclusion.

06 / ADVANCED REASONING

Midpoints, Menelaus, Ceva and homothety

Menelaus: in a triangle, a transversal through three side lines produces a product of directed side ratios equal to −1, or positive 1 using absolute lengths in a specified configuration. Ceva: three cevians are concurrent when their side ratios multiply to 1. Always identify triangle, transversal and placement.

Homothety: a dilation from a centre takes lines not through the centre to parallel lines and preserves angles. Externally tangent circles admit a negative homothety about the tangency point; this leads to a concise alternative solution for the 2019 perpendicular-chords problem.

When should you use these?

Try basic equal angles and similar triangles first. Use a transformation or a ratio theorem when it dramatically reduces the number of elements you need to track. Powerful named tools are not substitutes for checking hypotheses.

07 / PRACTICE

Four proof-building exercises

  1. Two chords AC and BD meet inside a circle at P. Prove PA·PC=PB·PD from two triangle similarities.
  2. A point H is the orthocentre of acute △ABC. Prove its reflection across BC lies on the circumcircle.
  3. If PA·PC=PB·PD for four distinct points on rays through P with the appropriate order, when can you deduce concyclicity? State the converse and the hypotheses.
  4. Explain why the claim “F lies on the given circle and on line ℓ” requires more than drawing F where you want it.
Solution outlines with logical checkpoints
  1. Use ∠PAB=∠PDC and ∠PBA=∠PCD, then similarity and corresponding ratios.
  2. Use BH⊥AC and CH⊥AB, then ∠BHC=180°−∠BAC and reflect.
  3. Choose the correct circle through three points and use the power-of-a-point converse; order and collinearity conditions matter.
  4. Define F as an intersection of ℓ with the circle, prove that intersection exists and meets any distinctness/placement condition, then proceed.
EXTENDED WORKSHOP 01 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

A robust circle-proof template

When to reach for this. Each angle equality should be followed by the theorem and by a check of which points lie on the common circle.

Complete worked argument

Let A,B,C,D lie on the same circle, in that order. Then ∠BAC and ∠BDC subtend the same chord BC, so they are equal when their vertices lie on the same arc segment; if on opposite arcs the corresponding unsigned angles are supplementary. Directed angles modulo 180 make the cyclic identity precise: ∠BAC≡∠BDC (mod 180) only after orientation is handled appropriately. Name the chord and the four points explicitly.

The tempting wrong moveDo not transfer an equality from a diagram if one vertex may lie on a different arc or extension. Directed angles or a clear case distinction are safer.

Transfer question

Two points X,Y on the same arc AB of a circle subtend chord AB. Prove ∠AXB=∠AYB.

Show a complete answer and proof

Both inscribed angles intercept the same remaining arc AB. By the inscribed-angle theorem, each equals half the central angle subtending that arc. Therefore they are equal. If X and Y instead occupy opposite arcs, the angles are supplementary.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 02 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

Similarity requires a vertex correspondence

When to reach for this. Many geometry errors come from the right picture and the wrong matching of triangle sides.

Complete worked argument

Suppose triangles ABC and DEF satisfy ∠A=∠D and ∠B=∠E. Then ∠C=∠F and △ABC∼△DEF with A↔D, B↔E, C↔F. Hence AB/DE=BC/EF=CA/FD. In a circle chord-intersection proof, first write down the two angle equalities from the same segments, then the exact triangle correspondence, and only then ratios.

The tempting wrong moveDo not take arbitrary products of lengths until you have established correspondence; a reversed ratio can create a false power-of-point identity.

Transfer question

Given △ABC∼△PQR with A↔P,B↔Q,C↔R and AB=6,BC=9,PQ=4, determine QR.

Show a complete answer and proof

AB/PQ=BC/QR gives 6/4=9/QR, so QR=6. The correspondence is essential.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 03 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

Power of a point from actual triangles

When to reach for this. You can prove the chord formula without memorising it if you identify two pairs of similar angles.

Complete worked argument

Let chords AB and CD intersect at an interior point P of one circle. Using equal angles in the same segment and vertical angles, triangles APC and DPB are similar: ∠APC=∠DPB, and ∠ACP=∠DBP. Therefore AP/DP=CP/BP, so AP·BP=CP·DP, after matching the correct segments of the intersecting lines. Equivalent conventional labels yield PA·PB=PC·PD.

The tempting wrong moveDo not apply the equation PA·PB=PC·PD when A,B,C,D do not lie on the same circle or P is not the intersection of the corresponding two lines.

Transfer question

From an external point T, a tangent TU touches a circle at U and a secant crosses it at A then B. Prove TU²=TA·TB.

Show a complete answer and proof

Apply the tangent–chord theorem to show △TUA∼△TBU. Corresponding ratios give TU/TA=TB/TU, so TU²=TA·TB. Note the tangent contact and collinearity of T,A,B.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 04 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

Why a reflected orthocentre lies on the circumcircle

When to reach for this. A standard orthocentre reflection is a useful transfer tool only if the perpendicularity and circle argument are shown.

Complete worked argument

In an acute triangle ABC with orthocentre H, reflect H across the line BC to obtain H′. Since BH makes angle 90°−∠C with BC and CH makes 90°−∠B with CB, reflection gives ∠BH′C=180°−∠A (from angle chase). Thus ∠BH′C+∠BAC=180°, so A,B,H′,C are concyclic. The same conclusion can also be deduced from directed-angle calculations for nonacute configurations where the reflected point is distinct.

The tempting wrong moveDo not assume “reflection preserves circles” is enough. Reflecting H alone across BC does not necessarily map the original circumcircle to itself; the cyclicity needs proof.

Transfer question

For a right triangle ABC with right angle A, where is the orthocentre and what happens when it is reflected in BC?

Show a complete answer and proof

The orthocentre is A itself because AB and AC are two altitudes. Reflect A across the hypotenuse BC. Since ∠A=90°, BC is a diameter of the circumcircle. The reflection A′ also satisfies ∠BA′C=90° (reflection preserves the right angle), so A′ lies on the same circumcircle by Thales’ converse.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 05 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

Ceva and Menelaus have different jobs

When to reach for this. Use Ceva for concurrency of three cevians and Menelaus for collinearity of three points on triangle sides or extensions.

Complete worked argument

In triangle ABC, points D on BC, E on CA, F on AB have AD,BE,CF concurrent if and only if (BD/DC)(CE/EA)(AF/FB)=1, for suitable interior nondegenerate positions. For a line through D,E,F on sides/extensions, Menelaus has a directed-product −1 version. One can derive Ceva by taking area ratios of triangles sharing a height and multiplying the three equalities.

The tempting wrong moveAvoid using the unsigned Ceva product in a configuration with exterior extensions without checking sign conventions and existence of intersections.

Transfer question

In triangle ABC, D is midpoint of BC, E midpoint of CA and F midpoint of AB. Prove the medians concur.

Show a complete answer and proof

Each of BD/DC, CE/EA and AF/FB equals 1. By Ceva, AD,BE,CF concur. This is valid for nondegenerate triangles with the chosen midpoints.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 06 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

Homothety handles tangent-circle configurations

When to reach for this. A homothety can replace several separate similar-triangle calculations when two circles touch.

Complete worked argument

Two externally tangent circles with centres O₁,O₂ meeting at P are related by a negative homothety about P, with scale −O₂P/O₁P. It maps the centre O₁ to O₂ and circles to circles. A line not through P maps to a parallel line, and a chord segment maps to a parallel chord. This is the structural idea behind a complete alternate proof of 2019 BMO1 Q3 in the marked companion.

The tempting wrong moveA negative homothety is not the same as a reflection across a line; it reverses directions through the centre, and its scale must carry the first radius to the second.

Transfer question

A homothety centred at S with scale k maps A to A′ and B to B′. Prove A′B′ is parallel to AB and its length equals |k|·AB.

Show a complete answer and proof

In vector form, A′=S+k(A−S), B′=S+k(B−S); subtract to get B′−A′=k(B−A). Thus their directions are parallel and lengths scale by |k|. No assumption about a diagram is required.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 07 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

Trigonometric area and sine-rule ratios

When to reach for this. Use area formula and sine rule when synthetic similarities are difficult, but keep the angle identities exact.

Complete worked argument

For any triangle ABC, area K=½bc sin A=½ca sin B=½ab sin C. Comparing the formulas shows a/sin A=b/sin B=c/sin C. If you need ratio AB/AC, it equals sin C/sin B. Many Ceva-related ratios follow from area identities, without calculating any degree angles.

The tempting wrong moveDo not use sin A/sin B=AB/AC without checking which sides are opposite A and B. Label side a opposite A consistently.

Transfer question

Prove the angle-bisector theorem with areas.

Show a complete answer and proof

If AD bisects ∠A and D lies on BC, [ABD]/[ACD]=BD/DC because both triangles share the altitude to BC. Their areas also equal ½AB·AD·sin BAD and ½AC·AD·sin DAC, so their ratio is AB/AC. Equate to conclude BD/DC=AB/AC.
