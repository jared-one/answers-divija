BENCHMARK 2 / FOLLOW-UP LEARNING

Finish the proof.

Three short repairs based on the kinds of final steps that matter most. These are not new examination questions. Try each prompt before opening its explanation.

A useful idea earns its place in a solution only when you explain what it implies, why it applies, and how it reaches the claim.
01 · ALGEBRA

From a candidate to a checked function

Suppose a functional equation suggests f(n) = n² + an + b for integer constants a,b.

Your task

What single substitution verifies the whole family for every permitted pair x,y in the equation f(x+y)+f(x−y)=2f(x)+2y²?

Reveal the completed verification

Substitute the proposed formula with arbitrary integers x,y.

f(x+y)+f(x−y)
= (x+y)²+a(x+y)+b
+ (x−y)²+a(x−y)+b
=2x²+2ax+2b+2y²
=2f(x)+2y²

This verifies sufficiency. A complete “find all” solution must separately establish that no other possible functions exist.

02 · NUMBER THEORY

Find a small multiple of a common divisor

Let d divide both n²+1 and (n+1)²+1. Subtraction shows d | (2n+1).

Your task

Use d | (n²+1) and d | (2n+1) to prove that d | 5. Then find exactly when the gcd is 5 rather than 1.

Reveal the proof
4(n²+1) − (2n−1)(2n+1) = 5

So d | 5 and d ∈ {1,5}. A gcd of 5 occurs precisely when n ≡ 2 (mod 5). Indeed n²+1 ≡ 0 (mod 5) iff n ≡ ±2, and (n+1)²+1 ≡ 0 (mod 5) selects n ≡ 2.

03 · COMBINATORICS

A construction is only half of optimality

Suppose you have a valid colouring of a 3-by-6 grid with two colours and no monochromatic rectangle. That proves six columns are possible, but does not prove seven columns are impossible.

Your task

Each column has three cells. By the pigeonhole principle two cells share a colour. Label one such pair by their row indices and colour. How many labels exist, and why may no label occur twice?

Reveal the upper bound

There are three row pairs and two colours, so only six possible labels. If seven columns existed, two columns would receive the same label. Those two rows would then have the same colour in both columns, creating a monochromatic rectangle. Thus at most six columns are possible, and the six-column construction shows the bound is sharp.

TRY THIS EVERY TIME

Five final checks

  1. Did I state the exact claim, including all quantifiers?
  2. Does each algebraic or geometric step follow from justified assumptions?
  3. If I proposed solutions, did I prove necessity and sufficiency?
  4. If I gave a construction, did I also prove no better one exists?
  5. Did I conclude precisely what the problem asks?
