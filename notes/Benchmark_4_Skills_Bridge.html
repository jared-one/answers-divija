01 / TRANSFER LESSON

Controlling a gcd

Reduce one polynomial modulo the other; then prove why a coprimality fact lets you cancel factors.

Short independent check

If gcd(r,s)=1, prove gcd(r+s,s²)=1.

Reveal the explanation

gcd(r+s,s)=gcd(r,s)=1. Every prime divisor of gcd(r+s,s²) would divide s and r+s, contradiction.

Try explaining the key move in your own words. Then choose a new problem that uses the same idea in a different disguise.

02 / TRANSFER LESSON

Two right angles and a circle

A point P lies on the circle with diameter UV exactly when ∠UPV=90°, provided P≠U,V. Once a cyclic quadrilateral is justified, angle relations and intersecting-secant products become available.

Short independent check

Let U,V,P,Q be concyclic. A line through X meets the circle at P,Q, and a second line at R,S. State the product identity.

Reveal the explanation

XP·XQ=XR·XS for the two secants, interpreting directed lengths if the point lies between intersections.

03 / TRANSFER LESSON

Pigeonhole with a useful invariant

The hard part is often identifying the boxes. Assign each object a feature that remains unchanged by the transformation you want to force.

Short independent check

Define the odd part of 24, 40, 45 and 72. Which two share the same odd part?

Reveal the explanation

Odd parts: 3,5,45,9 respectively. None share one; always check the examples before making a claim.

04 / TRANSFER LESSON

Symmetry and equality at the boundary

A symmetric polynomial inequality lets you reorder the variables for the proof. Nonnegativity constraints include zeros, which often produce additional equality patterns.

Short independent check

For x,y≥0, prove x²+y²≥2xy and identify every equality case.

Reveal the explanation

(x−y)²≥0. Equality exactly when x=y; check x=y=0 is included.

05 / TRANSFER LESSON

Angles in an orthic triangle

Feet of altitudes create cyclic quadrilaterals with a side as diameter. Angles at the circumference can transform an unfamiliar triangle into angle bisectors.

Short independent check

In acute triangle ABC, let D be the foot of the A-altitude and E the foot of the B-altitude. Why are A,B,D,E concyclic?

Reveal the explanation

∠ADB=∠AEB=90°, hence D and E lie on the circle with diameter AB.

06 / TRANSFER LESSON

Functions on every integer

Substituting special values is only the opening. A recurrence valid for every integer must be solved in both directions; verify every candidate in the original equation.

Short independent check

Let g:ℤ→ℤ satisfy g(n+1)−g(n)=5 for all n and g(0)=2. Find g(n), including negative n.

Reveal the explanation

g(n)=5n+2 for all integers n; use forward and backward differences to prove it.

OPEN CEILING / OPTIONAL EXTENSIONS

Four questions beyond the scored paper

These are not worth marks on Benchmark 4. Choose one if you want another layer of difficulty after completing the diagnostic.

  1. For coprime positive integers a,b, determine gcd(a+b,a²+b²).
  2. For every integer n≥1, prove that choosing n+1 distinct members of {1,…,2n} guarantees a pair where one divides the other. Construct n members with no such pair.
  3. Remove the requirement that f(n)≥0 from the functional-equation problem. Classify all integer-valued functions instead.
  4. Remove the normalisation a+b+c=1 from the inequality problem and investigate all equality cases for nonnegative triples. What happens at (0,0,0)?
See proof outlines after an independent attempt
  1. Reduce a²+b² modulo a+b to 2b²; coprimality gives gcd(a+b,2).
  2. Use the n odd parts 1,3,…,2n−1. For sharpness select n+1,…,2n.
  3. The quadratic subtraction gives f(n)=n²+cn for any integer c; each solves the equation, even when negative values occur.
  4. The inequality is homogeneous cubic, so scaling removes the sum restriction for nonzero triples. Equality holds when all three values are equal, or one is zero and the other two are equal (including the zero triple).
