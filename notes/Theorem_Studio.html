P01 / PROOF CRAFT / FOUNDATION

Contrapositive equivalence

Exact statement

To prove P ⇒ Q, it is logically equivalent to prove not Q ⇒ not P.

Complete reasoning

P ⇒ Q fails exactly when P is true and Q false. In that same case, not Q is true and not P false, so not Q ⇒ not P fails. Conversely in every other truth assignment both conditionals hold. Thus the statements are equivalent. This permits proofs by assuming the conclusion is false and showing the hypothesis cannot hold.

Try a transfer question

Prove if n² is odd, then n is odd.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Contrapositive: if n is even, n=2k and n²=4k² is even. Therefore n² odd implies n odd.

P02 / PROOF CRAFT / FOUNDATION

Pigeonhole principle

Exact statement

N objects placed into k boxes with N>k force two objects into one box.

Complete reasoning

Suppose at most one object lay in each box. Then k boxes could contain at most k objects, contrary to N>k. More generally if N>k(r−1), one box contains at least r objects, for otherwise all boxes together could hold at most k(r−1). The proof is a counting contradiction, not a guess about which box is full.

Try a transfer question

Choose five integers. Show two have difference divisible by four.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Place each of five integers in its residue class modulo4; four boxes. Two share a class, so their difference is 0 modulo4.

P03 / PROOF CRAFT / BRIDGE

Infinite descent principle

Exact statement

If every putative positive integer counterexample produces a strictly smaller positive counterexample, none exists.

Complete reasoning

Assume at least one positive counterexample exists. By well-ordering, choose the smallest positive counterexample n. The stipulated construction produces a positive counterexample m<n, contradicting minimality. This is equivalent in spirit to strong induction, though the contradiction viewpoint often suits Diophantine problems. A descent must be proved strictly decreasing and bounded below in the integers.

Try a transfer question

Why is “replace n by n/2” not automatically a valid descent?

Reveal a proof or complete answer

It may not be an integer; for odd n, n/2 is not an integer. A valid infinite descent needs a strictly smaller counterexample within the same positive-integer domain.

N01 / NUMBER THEORY / FOUNDATION

Euclidean algorithm

Exact statement

gcd(a,b)=gcd(b,r) whenever a=qb+r.

Complete reasoning

Any integer d dividing a and b also divides r=a−qb. Conversely any divisor of b and r divides a=qb+r. Thus the sets of common divisors coincide, so their greatest positive elements agree. Repeating with smaller nonnegative remainders terminates, since the remainder strictly decreases until zero. The final nonzero remainder is the gcd.

Try a transfer question

Find gcd(84,30) with a proof.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

84=2·30+24; 30=1·24+6; 24=4·6. Hence gcd(84,30)=gcd(30,24)=gcd(24,6)=6.

N02 / NUMBER THEORY / BRIDGE

Bézout identity

Exact statement

For integers a,b not both zero, gcd(a,b) is an integer linear combination of a and b.

Complete reasoning

Consider positive numbers expressible as xa+yb with integers x,y. By well-ordering choose the smallest positive such d. Divide a by d with remainder r. Since r=a−qd is also a linear combination and 0≤r<d, minimality forces r=0. Similarly d divides b. Since every common divisor of a,b divides every linear combination, d is the gcd. This yields both existence and coprimality criteria.

Try a transfer question

Show gcd(17,6)=1 by finding a linear combination.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

17−3·6=−1, or (−1)·17+3·6=1, so every common divisor divides1. Thus gcd=1.

N03 / NUMBER THEORY / BRIDGE

Euclid’s lemma

Exact statement

If prime p divides ab, then p divides a or p divides b.

Complete reasoning

If p divides a, done. Otherwise gcd(p,a)=1 because p is prime. Bézout gives up+va=1 for integers u,v. Multiply by b: upb+vab=b. Both terms on the left are divisible by p (the first obviously and the second because p|ab), so p|b. The primality is essential for deducing coprimality when p does not divide a.

Try a transfer question

Why does the statement fail if p is replaced by composite 6?

Reveal a proof or complete answer

6 divides 2·3, but 6 divides neither 2 nor3. Primality is needed.

N04 / NUMBER THEORY / BRIDGE

Fermat’s little theorem

Exact statement

For prime p and integer a not divisible by p, a^(p−1)≡1 (mod p).

Complete reasoning

Multiplication by a permutes the nonzero residue classes 1,…,p−1 modulo p because a is invertible modulo p. Multiplying all congruences gives a^(p−1)(p−1)!≡(p−1)! (mod p). The factorial is coprime to p, so it can be cancelled legitimately, proving the claim. The related formula a^p≡a (mod p) also holds when p|a.

Try a transfer question

Compute 2^100 mod7 without a calculator.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

2^3=8≡1 mod7, so 2^99=(2^3)^33≡1 and 2^100≡2 mod7.

N05 / NUMBER THEORY / ADVANCED

Chinese remainder theorem (two coprime moduli)

Exact statement

If gcd(m,n)=1, x≡a (mod m), x≡b (mod n) has a unique solution modulo mn.

Complete reasoning

Bézout gives um+vn=1. Set x=b·um+a·vn. Mod m, um≡0 and vn≡1, so x≡a. Mod n, um≡1 and vn≡0, so x≡b. If x and y are two solutions, both m and n divide x−y; because they are coprime, mn divides x−y. Thus uniqueness modulo mn.

Try a transfer question

Solve x≡2 mod3 and x≡3 mod5.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

x=2+3k; modulo5, 3k≡1 so k≡2 mod5; x≡8 mod15. Check 8≡2 mod3, 8≡3 mod5.

N06 / NUMBER THEORY / ADVANCED

Legendre’s valuation formula

Exact statement

The exponent of prime p in n! is Σ floor(n/p^k) over positive k.

Complete reasoning

Every multiple of p contributes at least one p to n!, giving floor(n/p) factors. Multiples of p² contribute at least a second factor, giving floor(n/p²) more, and so on. Every individual prime factor of p arising anywhere in the product is counted exactly once at its depth. The sum terminates when p^k>n.

Try a transfer question

Find the exponent of 3 in 30!.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

floor(30/3)+floor(30/9)+floor(30/27)=10+3+1=14, so v₃(30!)=14.

N07 / NUMBER THEORY / BRIDGE

Infinitely many primes

Exact statement

There is no finite complete list of prime numbers.

Complete reasoning

If primes were p₁,…,pₖ, set N=p₁p₂⋯pₖ+1>1. None of the listed primes divides N because it leaves remainder1. Yet every integer greater than1 has at least one prime divisor. Such a divisor is not on the list, a contradiction. N need not itself be prime.

Try a transfer question

Is 2·3·5·7+1 guaranteed to be prime?

Reveal a proof or complete answer

No. 211 happens to be prime, but the general construction need not give a prime. What the proof guarantees is a prime divisor not among the finite list.

A01 / ALGEBRA / FOUNDATION

The zero-product property

Exact statement

If xy=0 over the real numbers, at least one factor must be zero.

Complete reasoning

Suppose xy=0 and x≠0. Dividing the equation by nonzero x gives y=0. Thus x=0 or y=0. The reverse implication is immediate. This justifies factoring polynomial equations into alternatives, but division by a variable before handling its zero case loses solutions.

Try a transfer question

Find all real x with x(x−4)=0.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

The factorisation gives x=0 or x−4=0, so x=0 or 4. Both satisfy the original equation.

A02 / ALGEBRA / BRIDGE

Cauchy–Schwarz inequality

Exact statement

For real vectors u,v, (Σuᵢvᵢ)²≤(Σuᵢ²)(Σvᵢ²), equality iff proportional (including zero cases).

Complete reasoning

If v is nonzero, consider the nonnegative quadratic Σ(uᵢ−tvᵢ)² with t=(Σuᵢvᵢ)/(Σvᵢ²). Expanding and using nonnegativity yields Σuᵢ²−(Σuᵢvᵢ)²/Σvᵢ²≥0, which is the inequality. Equality holds exactly when every uᵢ=tvᵢ. If v=0, both sides vanish.

Try a transfer question

Prove a²+b²+c²≥(a+b+c)²/3 for reals.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Apply Cauchy to (a,b,c) and (1,1,1): (a+b+c)²≤3(a²+b²+c²). Equality iff a=b=c.

A03 / ALGEBRA / BRIDGE

AM–GM for two nonnegative numbers

Exact statement

For x,y≥0, (x+y)/2≥√xy, with equality iff x=y.

Complete reasoning

Because x,y≥0, their square roots exist. The square (√x−√y)²≥0 expands to x+y−2√xy≥0. Rearranging yields the bound. Equality holds exactly when the square is zero, i.e. x=y. This elementary proof also shows the hypothesis x,y≥0 is necessary for the root expression.

Try a transfer question

Given nonnegative x,y with x+y=12, find max xy.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

AM–GM gives √xy≤6, so xy≤36. Equality x=y=6 attains36; hence the maximum is36.

A04 / ALGEBRA / BRIDGE

Vieta’s formulas for quadratics

Exact statement

For roots r,s of x²−Sx+P, we have r+s=S and rs=P.

Complete reasoning

Expanding (x−r)(x−s)=x²−(r+s)x+rs and comparing coefficients with x²−Sx+P proves the identities. Conversely, any r,s with those sum and product form roots of that quadratic. When roots are integers and P≠0, each is a signed divisor of P.

Try a transfer question

Find integer roots of x²−7x+12=0.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Sum7, product12 ⇒ roots3 and4. Factor (x−3)(x−4)=0 and verify both.

A05 / ALGEBRA / ADVANCED

Rearrangement by swapping inversions

Exact statement

For sorted a₁≤⋯≤aₙ and b₁≤⋯≤bₙ, matching the same order maximises Σaᵢb_{π(i)}.

Complete reasoning

Suppose some permutation has an inversion i<j with b_{π(i)}>b_{π(j)}. Swapping these assigned b values changes the sum by (aⱼ−aᵢ)(b_{π(i)}−b_{π(j)})≥0. Repeatedly remove inversions until matching same order. Each swap does not decrease the sum, proving optimality. Reversing order yields the minimum. This requires real sorted sequences, not positivity.

Try a transfer question

For a=(1,2,3), b=(4,5,6), find max Σaᵢb_{π(i)}.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Same order gives1·4+2·5+3·6=32. Rearrangement proves no permutation can exceed32.

A06 / ALGEBRA / BRIDGE

Induction principle

Exact statement

A base case P(1) and an implication P(n)⇒P(n+1) for every positive n establish all P(n).

Complete reasoning

Assume some positive n violates the claim, and choose the least such n. It cannot be1 by the base case. Then n−1≥1 satisfies P(n−1) by minimality, so the induction step implies P(n), contradiction. This is a well-ordering proof of induction and shows why both parts are logically essential.

Try a transfer question

Prove 1+⋯+n=n(n+1)/2 by induction.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Base n=1. If true at n, add n+1 to obtain n(n+1)/2+(n+1)=(n+1)(n+2)/2. Thus the formula holds at n+1 and for all positive n.

C01 / COMBINATORICS / FOUNDATION

Stars and bars

Exact statement

The number of nonnegative integer solutions to x₁+⋯+x_k=n is C(n+k−1,k−1).

Complete reasoning

Represent n identical stars in a row and insert k−1 dividers, allowing adjacent dividers or dividers at the ends. The number of stars in each of the k intervals determines xᵢ. Conversely each solution gives exactly one such string of n stars and k−1 bars, a bijection. There are n+k−1 positions, so choose those occupied by bars.

Try a transfer question

Count ordered triples of nonnegative integers summing to5.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

C(5+3−1,3−1)=C(7,2)=21. The triples are ordered; identical stars encode multiplicity, not freely rearranged labels.

C02 / COMBINATORICS / FOUNDATION

Inclusion–exclusion for two sets

Exact statement

|A∪B|=|A|+|B|−|A∩B|.

Complete reasoning

When adding |A|+|B|, every element in A∩B has been counted twice, while all other elements in the union were counted once. Subtracting the intersection removes exactly one duplicate of each overlap, leaving every element counted exactly once. The same principle generalises to three sets by adding back their triple intersection.

Try a transfer question

How many numbers in 1..60 are divisible by2 or5?

Reveal a proof or complete answer

30 are multiples of2, 12 of5, six of lcm(2,5)=10. Total 30+12−6=36.

C03 / COMBINATORICS / BRIDGE

Handshaking lemma

Exact statement

In an undirected graph, Σv deg(v)=2|E|, so the number of odd-degree vertices is even.

Complete reasoning

Count vertex-edge incidences in two ways. Each edge has two endpoints and contributes two incidences, hence 2|E| total. Alternatively each vertex contributes deg(v) incidences. Equality follows. Since the sum of the degrees is even, the number of odd terms is even (an odd number of odd integers would have odd sum).

Try a transfer question

Can exactly three vertices have odd degree in any undirected finite graph?

Reveal a proof or complete answer

No. The handshaking lemma says the sum of degrees is even, hence the number of odd-degree summands must be even.

C04 / COMBINATORICS / ADVANCED

Erdős–Szekeres monotone subsequences

Exact statement

Every sequence of (r−1)(s−1)+1 distinct real numbers has an increasing subsequence of length r or a decreasing one of length s.

Complete reasoning

Assume neither exists. At each position i define pᵢ and qᵢ as longest increasing and decreasing subsequence lengths ending at i. Then 1≤pᵢ≤r−1 and 1≤qᵢ≤s−1. There are only (r−1)(s−1) pairs but more positions, so two indices i<j have the same pair. If xᵢ<xⱼ, an increasing subsequence ending at i extends to j and forces pⱼ≥pᵢ+1; otherwise a decreasing one extends and forces qⱼ≥qᵢ+1. Either contradicts equality of the pairs.

Try a transfer question

Why do ten distinct terms force an increasing or decreasing subsequence of four?

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Set r=s=4, so (r−1)(s−1)+1=3·3+1=10. Apply the theorem; nine terms can avoid both, demonstrating sharpness.

C05 / COMBINATORICS / ADVANCED

Ramsey R(3,3)=6

Exact statement

Among six people are either three mutual friends or three mutual strangers.

Complete reasoning

Represent each person by a vertex; colour each pair red for friendship or blue otherwise. At one vertex v, five edges lead out, so at least three share a colour. Suppose they connect v to a,b,c in red. If any pair among a,b,c shares a red edge, v and that pair form a red triangle. Otherwise all three edges among a,b,c are blue, making a blue triangle. Thus six suffice. Five can be coloured by a red 5-cycle and blue complementary 5-cycle, with no monochromatic triangle.

Try a transfer question

What role does the pigeonhole principle play in this proof?

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Of five incident edges in two colours, at least ceil(5/2)=3 share one colour. That triple drives the remaining case split.

C06 / COMBINATORICS / ADVANCED

Mantel’s theorem

Exact statement

A simple triangle-free graph on n vertices has at most floor(n²/4) edges, attained by a balanced complete bipartite graph.

Complete reasoning

For every edge uv, the neighbour sets N(u),N(v) are disjoint (a common neighbour would form a triangle), hence d(u)+d(v)≤n. Sum over all edges: Σv d(v)²≤n|E|, because vertex v contributes its degree d(v) once per incident edge. By Cauchy, Σv d(v)²≥(Σv d(v))²/n=(2|E|)²/n. Combining gives 4|E|²/n≤n|E|. For |E|>0, |E|≤n²/4; as an integer, at most floor(n²/4). A complete bipartite graph with parts of sizes floor(n/2),ceil(n/2) is triangle-free and has exactly that many edges.

Try a transfer question

What is the maximum number of edges in a triangle-free graph with n=7?

Reveal a proof or complete answer

floor(49/4)=12, attained by K₃,₄. The proof of the theorem establishes no larger graph is possible.

G01 / GEOMETRY / BRIDGE

Angle-bisector theorem

Exact statement

If AD bisects ∠BAC in triangle ABC and D lies on BC, then BD/DC=AB/AC.

Complete reasoning

Triangles ABD and ACD share the altitude from A to line BC, so their area ratio is BD/DC. They also have area (AB·AD·sin∠BAD)/2 and (AC·AD·sin∠DAC)/2, respectively. Because AD bisects the angle, the sines are equal, so the area ratio is AB/AC. Equating the two expressions proves the theorem.

Try a transfer question

If AB=6, AC=9 and BD=4, find DC given AD bisects angle A.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

BD/DC=AB/AC=6/9=2/3, so 4/DC=2/3 and DC=6.

G02 / GEOMETRY / BRIDGE

Power of a point for intersecting chords

Exact statement

If chords AB and CD meet at P inside a circle, PA·PB=PC·PD.

Complete reasoning

Using the inscribed angle theorem, triangles APC and DPB have ∠PAC=∠PDB and ∠ACP=∠DBP, while their angles at P are vertical. Hence they are similar with corresponding sides PA↔PD and PC↔PB. Thus PA/PD=PC/PB, so PA·PB=PC·PD. The proof explicitly uses all four points on one circle and the two intersecting lines; it is not a diagram guess.

Try a transfer question

If PA=3,PB=12,PC=4, find PD.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

PA·PB=PC·PD ⇒3·12=4·PD, so PD=9.

G03 / GEOMETRY / BRIDGE

Ceva’s concurrency theorem

Exact statement

For D∈BC, E∈CA, F∈AB in the interior of nondegenerate △ABC, the cevians AD,BE,CF concur iff (BD/DC)(CE/EA)(AF/FB)=1.

Complete reasoning

For concurrent cevians meeting at interior P, compare areas of △PAB, △PBC and △PCA. Ratios of areas of triangles with shared altitude to each triangle side give BD/DC=[PAB]/[PAC], CE/EA=[PBC]/[PAB] and AF/FB=[PAC]/[PBC]; multiplying cancels to1. Conversely if the product is1, let P=AD∩BE, take F′=CP∩AB; applying the forward implication to D,E,F′ shows AF′/F′B=AF/FB. Uniqueness of the point on AB with the given ratio gives F′=F, so CF also passes through P.

Try a transfer question

Show the three medians of a triangle concur using Ceva.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Each median meets the opposite side at its midpoint, so all three ratios in Ceva equal1; their product is1, proving concurrency.

G04 / GEOMETRY / BRIDGE

Cyclic quadrilateral criterion

Exact statement

A nondegenerate quadrilateral ABCD is cyclic iff a pair of opposite interior angles sums to 180°.

Complete reasoning

If the vertices lie on a circle in order, ∠ABC and ∠ADC subtend complementary arcs AC; their measures are half the respective central-arc measures, which sum to360°, hence the inscribed angles sum to180°. Conversely, through A,B,C construct the unique circle; its second intersection with line AD has supplementary angle at D. By the prescribed angle and uniqueness of the intersection geometry, D must coincide with that point. This uses a valid circle configuration, not visual measurement.

Try a transfer question

Is a rectangle cyclic? Prove using the criterion.

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Every interior angle is90°, so opposite interior angles sum to180°. By the converse cyclic-quadrilateral criterion, its four vertices lie on one circle.

G05 / GEOMETRY / ADVANCED

Homothety maps lines to parallel lines

Exact statement

A homothety centred at S with factor k≠0 maps segment AB to a segment parallel to AB, with length scaled by |k|.

Complete reasoning

In vector notation A′=S+k(A−S), B′=S+k(B−S). Subtracting yields B′−A′=k(B−A). Thus B′−A′ is a scalar multiple of B−A, establishing parallelism, and taking lengths gives A′B′=|k|AB. A negative factor reverses direction; this is useful for externally tangent circles viewed from their point of tangency.

Try a transfer question

A factor −2 maps a 3 cm segment not through the centre. What happens?

Reveal a proof or complete answer

Its image is parallel to the original, reverses direction as a directed segment, and has length6 cm.
