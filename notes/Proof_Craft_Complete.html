PROOF WRITING / COMPLETE COURSE

Proof craft: strategy, completion and review

Not a list of tricks: an applied system for choosing a method, writing the whole argument and learning from both correct and incomplete attempts.

Benchmark 3 detailed markingMicro-exams
01 / THE PURPOSE

What an olympiad proof must deliver

There are two different questions: “Did I find a promising mathematical idea?” and “Did I prove the exact statement?” The first is useful for learning; the second determines full proof credit.

Ask whether the task is a classification, existence proof, inequality, extremal bound, construction or geometric identity. Each has its own completion obligation:

Classification

Show every solution appears on the list, and every candidate works.

Extremal value

Show no better value is possible and show your proposed value is attainable.

Geometry

Identify the exact configuration and theorem, not an observed diagram property.

Invariant

Prove it survives every legal move, then apply it to initial and final states.

02 / CRITICAL REVIEW

Why partial credit can remain low

In proof-based mathematics, a good intuition is not a nearly complete proof. BMO marking uses a judgement of the whole argument, with substantial credit for a finished correct proof and limited credit for disconnected observations. Our private diagnostic marking is not official UKMT marking and seven-mark Benchmarks are not difficulty-equivalent to BMO ten-mark questions.

An unreadable line may need clarification from the original sheet; it is not evidence of a zero ability level. Conversely, the examiner cannot award points for a proof the candidate might have intended but did not present.

A constructive review format is: what was established → the first missing obligation → the correction → a new transfer problem. Marks are information about a particular script, not a measure of intelligence.

03 / EXAM STRATEGY

Pacing for a 3½-hour six-question paper

Start with a brief scan of all six problems. Choose based on a concrete entry point and a plausible route to completion, not just a familiar topic. A complete early question may earn more than scattered fragments across the paper.

Use a decision checkpoint when a path stalls: write the exact missing lemma; test whether it is true in small cases; decide whether to simplify, switch or postpone. This avoids spending two hours expanding expressions that never lead to a bound.

During the final writing stage, use short logical paragraphs. Reserve time to verify signs, assumptions, boundary cases and the final conclusion.

A flexible suggested strategy
  1. 8–12 minutes: scan and select.
  2. 35–50 minutes: attack the most accessible proof with full effort.
  3. At each natural pause: record useful lemmas, then choose whether to continue or move on.
  4. Finish: rewrite the strongest argument cleanly. Do not treat these times as strict mandates.
04 / LEARNING SCIENCE

A slow successful solution is not wasted

A long independent solve may involve generating conjectures, rejecting false routes, learning where a theorem applies, and writing a robust proof. These are real learning activities. A fast memorised solution can be less transferable than a slow, well-understood one.

To learn from a solved question, do a three-pass review: (1) state the central move in one sentence; (2) reproduce the proof without notes; (3) try a related but non-identical problem later. This trains retrieval and transfer, not just speed.

Because the examination is close, introduce timed practice only as a choice. Protect sleep and avoid assigning a full paper when exhausted.

05 / FROM SCRATCH

The five-question proof repair checklist

  1. What is given, including domain, positivity and distinctness?
  2. What exact theorem or claim is the destination?
  3. Which intermediate statement have I actually established?
  4. What unproved implication connects it to the destination?
  5. Have I checked all cases and the reverse direction, if required?

This checklist is more useful than “read more theorems” when a candidate already knows several mathematical ideas but loses marks on proof completeness.

06 / TEMPLATES

Reusable proof structures

Proof by contradiction

Assume the negation. Show a conflict with a given condition, a known theorem or an arithmetic impossibility.

Proof by induction

Base case. Induction hypothesis. Derive next case. Close the quantifier.

Pigeonhole

Define objects. Define buckets. Count them. Conclude collision. Translate collision to the claim.

Find all

Necessary restriction. Exhaustive candidates. Verify each candidate. Final classification.

Geometry similarity

Name triangles. Prove corresponding angles. State vertex correspondence. Deduce ratios. Close.

Sharpness

Upper/lower bound for all objects. Explicit extremal construction. Verify it meets every condition.

07 / SELF-CHECK

A disciplined five-minute final audit

Circle every division and confirm the denominator is nonzero. Underline equality claims; check when they actually occur. Inspect cases for exhaustive coverage. In geometry, confirm angle orientation and the existence of new points. In number theory, confirm parity/modular cancellation. In combinatorics, verify no object is counted twice or omitted.

Then write one closing sentence answering the original question in its own language. “The expressions seem to work” is not the same as “these are all solutions”.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 01 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

What to write in the first three lines

When to reach for this. The first line of a strong proof identifies the domain and the intended conclusion; this prevents accidental proof of a different statement.

Complete worked argument

For a classification, begin: “Suppose x,y are integers satisfying the equation.” Then derive a necessary factorisation or restriction. Only after exhausting possibilities present candidates. Finally write: “Conversely, each listed pair satisfies the original equation.” This last sentence is not ceremony: it completes the opposite implication.

The tempting wrong moveLong symbolic expansions without a named target make it difficult to see whether a step is relevant or sufficient.

Transfer question

Rewrite the opening of a proof finding all integer pairs with xy=6.

Show a complete answer and proof

Let x,y∈ℤ and xy=6. Then x is a signed divisor of6 and y=6/x. Since signed divisors are ±1,±2,±3,±6, these give all candidate pairs. Substitution shows each works, so the list is exhaustive.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 02 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

Recognise proof completion versus useful partial work

When to reach for this. Two equally creative ideas can receive different marks if one reaches the stated theorem and the other never crosses its final logical gap.

Complete worked argument

A counting example with nine items avoiding a pattern proves only that a universal threshold must be at least ten; a proof that ten items always force the pattern establishes the upper bound. Combining them gives the sharp threshold. By contrast, a clever counterexample alone cannot prove the ten-item assertion.

The tempting wrong move“I found the correct answer by experiment” is not the same as proving no counterexample exists. This distinction is particularly important in BMO marking.

Transfer question

What two claims must be established to prove that a maximum is exactly M?

Show a complete answer and proof

(1) A universal upper bound: every allowed configuration has value≤M. (2) Existence/attainment: construct an allowed configuration with value exactly M. Neither direction alone proves optimality.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 03 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

Decision points when a solution stalls

When to reach for this. A pause to identify the missing lemma can prevent two hours of algebra that never creates a justified upper bound.

Complete worked argument

If the current method is expanding a large polynomial, stop and ask whether its sign is apparent. Test equality cases, normalise using given constraints, look for Cauchy, AM–GM, Jensen-like elementary bounds or squared differences. If the new expansion introduces more terms but no clear route to the target, switch deliberately. Record the valuable facts you discovered first.

The tempting wrong moveA long attempt is not failure. The goal is to improve how quickly you detect whether an argument has a plausible path to completion while preserving deep reasoning.

Transfer question

You want to show (a²+b²+c²)²≤a³+b³+c³ for a+b+c=1 and a,b,c≥0. What question should be asked before expanding?

Show a complete answer and proof

Try Cauchy with vectors (a^{3/2},b^{3/2},c^{3/2}) and (a^{1/2},b^{1/2},c^{1/2}). The left side becomes the squared inner product, and the right side the product of squared lengths. Check the nonnegativity condition and then the equality cases.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 04 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

A clean proof is compressed reasoning, not missing reasoning

When to reach for this. Olympiad write-ups can be short and rigorous when each crucial implication is visible and its conditions are stated.

Complete worked argument

One crisp proof of the contiguous-sum lemma: Let S₀=0 and Sⱼ=Σ_{i=1}^{j}aᵢ for j=1,…,n. Two among S₀,…,Sₙ have equal residue mod n, so Sⱼ−Sᵢ=Σ_{k=i+1}^{j}aₖ is divisible by n for some i<j. This is short because the key objects and pigeonhole justification are explicit.

The tempting wrong moveSaying only “pigeonhole principle” without naming the objects and buckets is not a complete proof.

Transfer question

Turn this into a full argument: “Among five integers two share a remainder mod4.”

Show a complete answer and proof

Five integers each occupy one of the four residue classes 0,1,2,3 modulo4. By pigeonhole, two occupy the same class. Their difference is therefore divisible by4. Every chosen integer is accounted for.

EXTENDED WORKSHOP 05 / READ, PROVE, TRANSFER

Diagnostic marks versus student identity

When to reach for this. The student deserves transparent feedback on the submitted script, not a claim about intelligence, mathematical ceiling or predicted university/IMO success.

Complete worked argument

A mark should record what is present on the page, point to the first missing proof obligation, supply a model repair and offer a fresh transfer exercise. A photograph with unreadable details should be marked as uncertain rather than confidently interpreted as zero. Past years, practice conditions, health and familiarity can all change scores, making isolated comparison unreliable.

The tempting wrong moveDo not promise a BMO2 qualification threshold based on historical awards. Rules change, and the 2027 BMO2 National Final is now invitation only.

Transfer question

A student scores 10/10 on a proof after previously missing a related question. Does this alone prove a fixed improvement of 10 marks on a future paper?

Show a complete answer and proof

No. It is good evidence of a successful proof on that task. To infer transfer or exam readiness, use multiple new problems under known comparable conditions and evaluate quality and time separately.
