01 / AUXILIARY LINES

What can a new point really do?

If you construct F on a circle, you may use the fact that F lies on that circle because you defined it that way. You may not also assume that F lies on some other line unless you construct it at that intersection and justify existence.

Your check

Write two sentences: one defining F exactly, and one explaining why an angle theorem applies.

02 / A WORKED STRATEGY TO REBUILD INDEPENDENTLY

Midpoints, trisection and one surprising right angle

This notes page references the earlier midpoint/trisection geometry example. The important bridge is to identify a midpoint inside the construction before using the supplied angle equality.

  1. Mark the original midpoint and trisection ratios.
  2. Let M be the intersection of the two constructed lines. Use Menelaus in the appropriate triangle to show that M divides a segment equally.
  3. Convert the given angle equality into an isosceles triangle, so that a second length equals that half-segment.
  4. Recognise three equal distances from M; a diameter/right-angle argument now completes the proof.
Reveal the key missing bridge

In the earlier configuration, the Menelaus ratios give DM=MC. The angle hypothesis gives AM=DM. Thus AM=DM=MC, so A,D,C lie on a circle centred at M with DC as a diameter. Thales' theorem gives the right angle.

This is a worked learning explanation, not a fresh unaided score. Reconstruct it from an unmarked diagram after closing the notes.

03 / CIRCLE GEOMETRY

A circle theorem needs four named points

For an acute triangle ABC and its orthocentre H, reflect H across side BC to H′. Instead of drawing many circles, aim to prove that ABH′C is cyclic.

Try to prove

∠BH′C + ∠BAC = 180°. Where are you using perpendicular altitudes, and where are you using reflection?

Reveal the final step
Reflection preserves ∠BHC, and the altitude-angle calculation gives ∠BHC=180°−∠BAC. Therefore the opposite angles of ABH′C are supplementary, proving the cyclic claim by its converse. Repeat with the other sides.
04 / SHORT RETEST

A three-minute proof checklist

What I may mark

Given equalities, proved perpendicular lines, declared points, and justified circle membership.

What I may not assume

A line passes through the centre because it appears to; two angles look equal; an intersection exists in every configuration.

Finish with a sentence: “By [the theorem], since [the proved condition], therefore [the exact required result].”
