Before opening the model solution

  1. Draw a clean figure and state exactly what must be shown.
  2. List only the hypotheses you are allowed to use; do not assume a diagram is to scale.
  3. Write one justified lemma, even if you cannot finish.
  4. Ask: Is this an existence problem, an angle relation, or a length relation?
  5. At the time boundary, preserve the partial proof. A hint is a separate learning condition, not a failed attempt.

03 / WORKFLOW AND PACING

Turn a question bank into proof progress.

Attempt

Hide the answer. Record problem ID, topic, time and whether you previously saw it. Explore honestly.

Audit

Separate the exact proposition proved from any conjecture. For a “find all” result, prove both necessity and sufficiency.

Transfer

After reading a hint or solution, close it. Reconstruct the proof and solve a nearby unfamiliar problem later.

Proof-completion checkpointDo not confuse seeing the right answer, finding a key idea, and writing a complete proof. All three can be useful, but only the third earns a complete proof mark.

04 / EVIDENCE AND MARKING

How to know whether a solution counts

MathsDB can organise practice and progress; completion of a practice page is not an official proof grade. Use the original contest marking report or official solution wherever available. Your Proof Desk companions show why steps earned or lost credit on particular submitted scripts.

Test yourself: what must a “find all” proof show?
You must prove that every proposed answer satisfies the original conditions and that no other answer is possible. Listing examples or one direction alone is insufficient.
Test yourself: what must an “if and only if” proof show?
Prove both P ⇒ Q and Q ⇒ P. An “only if” statement P only if Q is P ⇒ Q; an “if” statement P if Q is Q ⇒ P. Read the complete logic course for counterexamples and quantifiers.
Test yourself: does a slow correct solution teach anything?
Yes. Try to isolate the key idea, reduce the number of exploratory branches in the final proof, and reproduce the argument without notes. Speed is a skill to train after sound reasoning.
