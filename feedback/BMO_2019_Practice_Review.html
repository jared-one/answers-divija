OFFICIAL 2019 BMO1 / Q02

Integer sequence

A proof about an infinite sequence requires both a restriction and a way to continue indefinitely.

Before reopening the official answer

Transform the recurrence into (2an+1−an)²=1. Explain why even terms cannot occur.

Reveal the proof-check prompt
For odd an, the two successor candidates (an±1)/2 are consecutive integers, so one is odd. Repeating this choice produces an infinite sequence.

Compare the full argument with the official markers' report rather than treating this summary as the complete official proof.

OFFICIAL 2019 BMO1 / Q03

Tangent circles

A geometry argument can be short and strong when every angle relationship has a reason.

Before reopening the official answer

Mark centre-to-tangent perpendiculars, central angles, and angles at the circumference.

Reveal the proof-check prompt
Show the relevant angles sum to 90° and state precisely which theorem gives each equality. Do not assume a special orientation solely from your sketch.

OFFICIAL 2019 BMO1 / Q05

Sweets around a table

Finding that a construction works for multiples of 28 proves only one direction.

Before reopening the official answer

Prove “if 28 divides n, an arrangement exists” and also “if an arrangement exists, 28 divides n.”

Reveal the proof-check prompt
Do not infer necessity from a few failed examples. The second implication needs a structural restriction applying to every possible arrangement.

What official marking rewards

Strong evidence

A full argument with the critical implication, complete case coverage and the exact requested conclusion.

Limited evidence

Interesting numerical examples, isolated observations, or a construction with no proof that alternatives are impossible.
